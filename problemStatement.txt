1. The product in brief
Problem. People who want to keep a diary can't recall the day's moments by night. Entries end up thin, the habit feels pointless, and they quit.
Hypothesis (unvalidated). People remember things at the moment they happen. If they can add to their day's page in pieces, and the app turns long, messy writing into a polished page and a short story, they will keep a diary longer.
Primary user. People who tried a diary, forgot the day's details when they sat down to write, saw entries thin out, and quit.
One line. A clean personal daily diary: write or speak whenever something happens, and each day becomes a polished page with a heading and a one-page story.
Nature of this project. A learning and portfolio project. No launch, no real users. Privacy and security are out of scope by choice, but this is listed on the What's Next screen.
2. Design principles
1. A diary, not a dashboard. No stats, streaks, mood pickers, tag chips, or metric cards on the writing and reading screens.
2. Premium and calm. Ivory paper, ink serif text, one bronze accent, generous whitespace, hairline dividers, almost no shadows.
3. Never ask the user to fill in fields. The user writes or speaks. The AI does the rest.
4. The raw words are the source of truth. Everything the AI produces points back to what the user wrote.
5. Insights live in Memories only. Moods, ribbons, throwbacks, and recaps never appear on Home or on a day page.
6. The AI never invents facts. Polishing and summarizing may reword and condense. They may not add.
7. If in doubt, remove an element.
3. User journey (locked)
1. Open the app. Today's blank page appears with the date at the top and the capture options (type, speak, photo, video). Bottom nav: Home, Calendar, Memories, Profile.
2. Write in pieces through the day. Each piece you add is saved, then polished automatically (grammar fixed, wording improved, meaning unchanged).
3. The day's story forms quietly. After each piece, the app updates that day's heading and one-page story. There is no review or confirm step.
4. Read a day. From the Calendar, open a day to see the heading and the one-page story first. Tap a sentence to see the passage it came from. Tap Read everything I wrote for the full polished writing.
5. Add to any past day. From the Calendar, open a day and add to it. The piece is marked added later.
6. Memories. Throwbacks, weekly and monthly recaps, and marked days (Happiest day, Most travelled, and so on) live here. These also drive in-app notifications such as One year ago today.
7. Replay. Any day can be played as a short recap reel made from its photos and story.
8. Profile. The book's name, an optional evening reminder, notification toggles, export, a Load demo diary button, and a What's Next screen.

5. Screens
Stitch screens are a reference only. Cursor may select from them, borrow parts, or design its own screens, whichever best suits the app. The rules below always win over the reference.
Navigation: four tabs, Home, Calendar, Memories, Profile, with thin line icons.
1. Home (today's page). Date at the top. A blank writing page with a soft cursor and the placeholder What happened today? A slim capture bar at the bottom with type, speak, photo, video, and an Add button. Earlier pieces from today appear as quiet collapsed entries with timestamps above the new text.
2. Speaking. The page is dimmed, with a minimal sheet showing a waveform, a timer, and live words flowing into the page. Done and Discard.
3. Calendar. A month grid. Days with entries get a tiny bronze dot. Today is gently circled. Tapping a day opens it.
4. Day (story). The date, a short heading, and a one-page story in first person with a drop cap and an optional inline photo. Links at the bottom: Read everything I wrote and Add to this day. Tapping a sentence opens a soft panel with the source passage.
5. Everything I wrote. The polished pieces in order, with hairline dividers and timestamps, voice mark on dictated pieces, media inline, and an added later label where it applies. A Back to the story link.
6. Add to a past day. The same writing page as Home with that day's date and a small label, Adding to a past day.
7. Memories. Three sections: One year ago today (or the closest past day), This week (a short recap with quoted lines linking to days), and Marked days (a horizontal row of day cards with a small bronze label).
8. Marked day detail. A sheet with the ribbon name, two or three quoted lines from that day as evidence, and links: Read the day, Replay this day.
9. Monthly recap. A two-paragraph reflection with Pages worth returning to. No numbers or charts.
10. Replay. A full-screen slow slideshow with captions in white serif over a soft fade, a progress line, a close button, and a music toggle.
11. Profile. The book's name, evening reminder time, memory notifications toggle, writing style (Polished), Export my diary, Load demo diary, Clear all data, About, and What's Next.
12. What's Next. See section 10.
States to design: empty today, empty Calendar, story updating (a quiet line under the story, no spinner or modal), AI unavailable (show the polished or raw text and a quiet retry), no photos for Replay (text-only slides), and demo data loaded (a small unobtrusive label).
6. AI behavior specification
All AI calls go through one server route so the API key never ships to the browser. Every AI response is JSON, validated against a schema. Invalid responses are retried once, then fall back gracefully.
6.1 Polish (per piece)
• Input: the raw piece text and its timestamp.
• Task: fix spelling, grammar, and punctuation, improve clarity and phrasing into good, natural English, and keep the person's first-person voice.
• Hard rules: keep the meaning, names, numbers, places, and order. Do not add facts, feelings, or details. Do not make it more dramatic. Keep roughly the same length (it may shorten repetition).
• Storage: the raw text is saved unchanged in a hidden raw field. The polished text is shown. The user can tap Undo polish on any piece to revert to the raw text.
6.2 Day story (per day, updated after each new piece)
• Input: all of that day's polished pieces with IDs, in order, plus media descriptions if any.
• Output: { heading, story: [{ text, source_piece_ids }] }.
• Task: a short, warm, first-person story of about one page (3 to 4 short paragraphs for a long day, fewer for a short day). The heading is under 8 words and describes the day's real content.
• Hard rules: only use what the pieces say. Every sentence must cite at least one valid piece ID. Never invent facts, feelings, or events. A day with one short note gets one or two sentences, not padding.
• Validation: drop or regenerate any sentence with no valid source. If more than a third fail, keep the previous story and retry later.
• Triggers: debounced 8 seconds after the last added piece, and again when a past day gets a new piece.
6.3 Insights (per day, background)
• Input: the day's polished pieces.
• Output: { moods: [{ label, evidence_quotes }], ribbons: [{ label, strength, evidence_quotes }], places: [...], people: [...] }.
• Rules: labels are open-ended (Happiest day, Lots of fun, Met friends, Felt nostalgic, Most travelled, Craziest day, Hard day, Proud moment, and any others that fit). Evidence quotes must be exact substrings of the user's writing. Places and people come only from the text.
• Rarity: a ribbon is awarded only when the day stands out against the others. Recompute across days, and cap at about one ribbon per 7 days per label.
• Where it shows: Memories and its sheets only.
6.4 Recaps
• Weekly: the week's day stories in, a short two-paragraph letter-style recap out, with two or three quoted highlights linking to their days. Generated on demand, then cached.
• Monthly: the same, with Pages worth returning to (4 days with one quoted line each).
• Throwbacks: no AI needed. Pick the day from one year ago (or the nearest earlier memorable day) and show its heading and story excerpt.
6.5 Photos
If an image is attached without caption text, ask the vision model for one factual sentence about what is visible. It never guesses who or why. This sentence is stored as the media description.
6.6 Cost and limits
The free tier has rate limits, so queue calls, debounce them, cache results by content hash, and never block the UI. If a call fails, the app still works with the raw and polished text.

11. Acceptance criteria
• Opening the app shows today's blank page and the capture options within one second.
• A text piece can be added in under 5 seconds from opening the app.
• After a piece is added, polish appears within a few seconds, and Undo polish restores the raw words exactly.
• The polished text keeps names, numbers, places, and meaning. On 10 test pieces, none gains a fact that the raw text lacked.
• Every sentence of a day story opens a source passage. On 10 test days, none contains a fact absent from the pieces.
• A day with one short note produces a one or two sentence story, not padding.
• The Calendar shows a dot for each day with entries, and any past day can be added to, marked added later.
• Memories appears only after enough data exists, or instantly after Load demo diary.
• No insight, mood, or ribbon appears on Home, Calendar, or Day screens.
• Every ribbon shows exact quotes from the user's own writing.
• With the network off, the user can still write and read. AI features show a quiet unavailable state.
• The What's Next screen lists the features above, and I want this persists on the device.
• The app installs as a PWA, and its production build costs nothing to run on free tiers.
12. Measurement (kept for the portfolio story)
• North star: days per week with a saved diary page, per user.
• Capture: median time from opening the app to a saved piece, and pieces per active day.
• AI quality: the rate of Undo polish taps, and the rate of dismissed or incorrect ribbons.
• Implementation: a hidden, local-only debug page at /debug may show these numbers from the demo and the developer's own use. It is not part of the user app.
